## ✨ What Lipwise Actually Does

Most people type far slower than they speak. Lipwise closes that gap. You press and hold a keyboard shortcut, say what you want to say, and release. A moment later, clean, correctly punctuated text appears wherever your cursor happens to be — an email window, a chat box, a document, a search bar, a support ticket, a spreadsheet cell.

Three things make Lipwise different from a basic dictation tool:

**Speech recognition runs on your computer.** Your audio is converted to text locally rather than being shipped off to a transcription service. This keeps your voice data on your machine and keeps dictation responsive even on a slow connection.

**An AI editor cleans up what you said.** Raw speech is messy. We repeat ourselves, change our minds mid-sentence, and speak in fragments. Lipwise passes the raw transcript through an AI editing step that turns rambling speech into the sentence you actually meant.

**You choose which AI does the editing.** You can point Lipwise at a local AI model running on your own machine, or connect it to Claude or OpenAI. If privacy is your priority, run everything locally. If you want maximum polish, connect a cloud model.

## 🎯 Who Lipwise Is For

Lipwise is useful if you write a lot and would rather talk than type. That includes people writing emails and reports all day, people with repetitive strain injuries or wrist pain, people who think out loud better than they type, customer support staff answering the same kinds of questions repeatedly, students drafting essays, developers writing commit messages and documentation, and anyone who finds a keyboard slows down their thinking.

It is also useful if you work in multiple apps at once. Lipwise does not live inside one program. It types into whatever is focused on your screen, so you are not locked into a single editor or a browser tab.

- **Push-to-talk dictation.** Hold a shortcut, speak, release. No clicking buttons, no switching windows.
- **Works in any app.** Email clients, word processors, chat apps, browsers, note tools, code editors, forms and anything else with a text field.

## 🎙️ How to Dictate Day to Day

The routine is the same everywhere:

1. Click into the text field where you want words to appear.
2. Hold your Lipwise shortcut.
3. Speak naturally at a normal pace.
4. Release the shortcut.
5. Wait a moment while Lipwise transcribes the audio and applies the AI editing step.
6. Keep typing, or keep dictating.

A few habits make a large difference:

- **Speak in full thoughts.** Pause briefly between sentences rather than between words.
- **Do not slow down artificially.** Modern recognition handles normal conversational speed better than exaggerated, careful speech.
- **Say punctuation when you need it.** Words like “comma,” “period,” “question mark” and “new paragraph” are recognised.
- **Use “scratch that” freely.** If you fluff a sentence, say “scratch that” and start the sentence again. It is faster than deleting by hand.

## 🧠 Using the AI Editor

The AI editor is what separates Lipwise from plain dictation. Raw speech recognition gives you a literal transcript. The editor gives you writing.

Examples of instructions you can speak:

- “Scratch that, start over.”
- “Make that a bulleted list.”
- “Rewrite that more formally.”
- “Shorten this to two sentences.”
- “Turn this into an email to a client.”
- “Remove the filler words.”

The editor respects your intent rather than transcribing it literally. If you say “make that a list,” you get a list — the instruction itself does not appear in your document.

## ⚙️ Choosing an AI Provider

**Local AI** runs a model on your own computer. Nothing leaves your machine. This is the most private option and works without an internet connection. It needs more memory and may feel slightly slower on older hardware.

**Claude** handles complex editing instructions well and is a strong choice if you frequently dictate long, structured documents.

**OpenAI** offers a similar experience with a different balance of speed and writing style.

You can switch providers whenever you like. Your dictation history and settings stay with you.

## 🔒 Privacy and Your Voice

Speech recognition takes place on your computer. Your microphone audio is not uploaded to a transcription service. When you use local AI, both transcription and editing stay on your machine. If you connect Claude or OpenAI, the text of your dictation is sent to that provider for editing, and your audio still stays local.

## ❓ Troubleshooting

**Nothing appears when I release the shortcut.** Confirm your cursor is in an editable text field, and check that the correct microphone is selected in settings.

**The text is garbled or cuts off.** Move closer to the microphone, reduce background noise, and check that your shortcut is not being intercepted by another app.

**Dictation feels slow.** This is usually the AI editing step rather than recognition. Try a smaller local model, or switch to a cloud provider.

**My shortcut does nothing in one specific app.** Some apps capture keyboard shortcuts globally. Choose a different Lipwise shortcut combination.
